Photocell monitor

Each exit photocell checks for board twice as the sheet leaves the station: at the first and second distance from the sheet's front edge. Values 0 to 1300 mm, the longest sheet the machine takes in the feed direction. This job's sheet is 1050 mm long, so a check point past 1050 mm looks at empty space and trips. A BS photocell with no values of its own uses the AS values of its station. Only one photocell per station can be switched off, so the sheet is always seen. Front lays has one photocell and can be switched off. The Stripping exit photocells can be moved across the sheet to look at solid board instead of holes or slots.

Stack height is 400 mm max, so the most sheets per stack depends on the board: E 265, EF 173, B 147, C 99, EB 97, EC 72, BC 61. The lifting belt lowers every 5 sheets. At the full stack, small top forks catch the next sheets while the belt lowers, then the big lower forks take over while the stack drives out and the belt comes back up (about 4 s). The small forks hold about 5 sheets, so the feeder leaves empty cycles. The small forks stay in until the big forks are in place. The empty cycles give the last sheet time to land and the small forks time to move in: a sheet still falling while they move in jams, so higher speed needs more empty cycles.

The prefeeder keeps the G-Cut infeed stack full and stores board on its inclined belt. When the stack on the lifting bed is used up, the bed lowers, takes the next stack and lifts it again (about 25 s). Only the stored board feeds the G-Cut in that time: at 6,500 sheets/h of C flute that is about 7 mm of board per second, so too little stored board runs the infeed empty (E120). A forklift brings a new 1600 mm stack about 30 s after the waiting spot is empty. The prefeeder belt rises to 400 mm under the transfer belt, so the stored board plus the next bundle can be 400 mm at most: the next bundle only comes when it fits.

Simulate a fault

The fault goes on the next sheet that reaches that station. If its photocells are on, the machine stops before damage. If they are switched off, the fault gets through and jams further on.
